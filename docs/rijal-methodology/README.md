# Rijal methodology case studies

These studies test Usul16's evidence model against difficult, concrete cases. They keep
person identity, occurrence resolution, personal reliability, route topology and
report-level judgment separate. A conclusion is scoped to the evidence and occurrence it
actually addresses; it is not permission for a global name merge or blanket grading rule.

The six files were recovered from the August 2026 working tree and reviewed on 2026-10-05.
All are readable UTF-8 Markdown, contain a stated result, document evidence limits and end
in an adjudication or engine-facing conclusion. Their source arguments were preserved as
research records; the restart review did not independently re-adjudicate every cited edition.

| Case | Main engine lesson |
|---|---|
| [1 — Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl](cases/01_muhammad_b_ismail.md) | A recurrent occurrence can have a strong identity resolution while personal reliability remains unresolved. |
| [2 — Sahl b. Ziyād](cases/02_sahl_b_ziyad.md) | Conflicting early evidence and method-dependent later judgments must remain visible rather than becoming one neutral grade. |
| [3 — Abū Baṣīr](cases/03_abu_basir.md) | Person equivalence and the resolution of a bare kunya in one chain are different questions. |
| [4 — Ibrāhīm b. Hāshim](cases/04_ibrahim_b_hashim.md) | Cumulative reliability inference must expose its premises and avoid counting dependent evidence as independent attestations. |
| [6 — Mashyakha expansion](cases/06_mashyakha_expansion.md) | Literal, parsed and reconstructed isnads remain separate; connection does not itself authenticate every route-person. |
| [8 — Aṣḥāb al-Ijmāʿ](cases/08_ashab_al_ijma.md) | Person authentication, report-level convention and claims about omitted intermediaries require distinct fields and method-indexed conclusions. |

The numbering preserves the recovered working sequence. No case files numbered 5 or 7 were
present in the snapshot, so this index makes no completeness claim for those slots.
