# Case 4 — Ibrāhīm b. Hāshim: inferred tawthīq without an early explicit formula

**Research state:** case-study complete; primary-source and corpus audit dated 2026-08-24

**Entity under review:** إبراهيم بن هاشم أبو إسحاق القمي (Usul16 person `332`)
**Core problem:** how far may reception, transmission practice, a general preface, and cumulative contextual indicators substitute for an early biographical formula such as `ثقة`?

## 1. Result at a glance

No located early biographical notice calls Ibrāhīm b. Hāshim `ثقة` in so many words. Al-Najāshī and al-Ṭūsī instead preserve biographical and bibliographical facts: Kufan origin, settlement in Qum, books, a reported meeting with al-Riḍā, and—most importantly—the collective report that he was the first to disseminate Kufan ḥadīth in Qum. Al-ʿAllāma al-Ḥillī states the evidentiary problem exactly: `ولا على تعديله بالتنصيص`, yet prefers acceptance.

The later positive verdict is therefore **inferential**, but it is not evidence-free. Its strongest components are:

1. the statement in the extant preface to *Tafsīr al-Qummī* that the material comes through `مشايخنا وثقاتنا`, coupled with ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm's extensive transmission from his father;
2. the reported and observable reception of Ibrāhīm's material in Qum;
3. extraordinary transmission density and sustained narration by leading transmitters;
4. later scholars' treatment of routes containing him as acceptable or sound; and
5. the absence of an adverse notice despite prominence and scrutiny.

These are not five independent eyewitness attestations. Several are later interpretations of the same small set of upstream facts. A defensible system must therefore preserve both the **positive cumulative inference** and the **dependency discount**.

### Method-sensitive outcome

| Method | Defensible status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Early explicit-formula only | `no_explicit_tawthiq` / suspend | Neither al-Najāshī nor al-Ṭūsī supplies `ثقة`; silence is not jarḥ. |
| Accept a son's general certification of his direct mashāyikh | reliable | Ibrāhīm is overwhelmingly the father from whom ʿAlī transmits. |
| Cumulative qarāʾin | reliable with high confidence | Preface, Qummī reception, scale, recognized transmitters, and lack of criticism reinforce one another. |
| Al-Khūʾī's stated method | `thiqa` | He says there should be no doubt and gives three express grounds. |
| Al-Tustarī's source-critical presentation | positive evidence, narrower confidence statement | He attacks inflated bundles and calls the son's preface the best item; he does not supply a fresh early explicit formula. |
| If preface attribution/scope and reception inference are both rejected | principled tawaqquf remains possible | The remaining prominence and silence are strong contextual indicators, not a literal taʿdīl text. |

The database must not turn this into either `early explicit thiqa` or `weak`. The correct representation is a scholar- and method-indexed conclusion supported by individually addressable evidence records.

## 2. Scope, identity, and edition control

This case concerns the well-known Qummī transmitter and father of ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm. It does **not** merge him with `إبراهيم بن هاشم العباسي`, a separate or corrupt title discussed immediately after him in later works. The stable identity anchors are:

- kunya: أبو إسحاق;
- origin and migration: Kufa → Qum;
- son/student: ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm b. Hāshim;
- recurrent relational surface form: `علي بن إبراهيم، عن أبيه`;
- works: *al-Nawādir* and *Qaḍāyā Amīr al-Muʾminīn*.

Page numbers below are tied to the named digitized edition. Where a host's internal URL index differs from the printed page, both are stated. Editorial footnotes are separated from authorial text: this matters especially on al-Ṭūsī's *Fihrist* p. 36 and al-Ḥillī's *Khulāṣa* p. 49, whose modern footnotes reproduce later Khūʾī-style reasoning.

## 3. Earliest recoverable evidence

### 3.1 Al-Najāshī: facts, an upstream attribution, and an explicit reservation

Al-Najāshī, *Rijāl al-Najāshī*, ed. Mūsā al-Shubayrī al-Zanjānī, Muʾassasat al-Nashr al-Islāmī, Qum, entry 18, p. 16 ([exact page](https://usul.ai/ar/t/rijal-2/17); the host path is one greater than the printed page):

> `قال أبو عمرو الكشي: تلميذ يونس بن عبد الرحمن ... هذا قول الكشي، وفيه نظر`

He then reports:

> `وأصحابنا يقولون: أول من نشر حديث الكوفيين بقم هو`

and names *al-Nawādir* and *Qaḍāyā Amīr al-Muʾminīn* with a book-transmission route through ʿAlī from his father.

Evidence extraction:

| Proposition | Speaker / source layer | Evidentiary type | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student of Yūnus; among al-Riḍā's companions | al-Kashshī as quoted by al-Najāshī | attributed tabaqa report | No tawthīq; al-Najāshī expressly doubts it. |
| First to spread Kufan ḥadīth in Qum | unnamed `أصحابنا`, reported by al-Najāshī | collective reception/biography report | Does not literally say `ثقة`; later scholars infer acceptance. |
| Two named books and route | al-Najāshī | bibliographical/isnād evidence | A route to a book is not by itself personal taʿdīl. |

The words `هذا قول الكشي، وفيه نظر` attach to the Yūnus/companion claim, not to the dissemination report. A parser that extends the reservation to the entire entry would reverse the source.

### 3.2 Al-Ṭūsī's *Fihrist*: parallel biographical report, still no explicit tawthīq

Al-Ṭūsī, *al-Fihrist*, ed. Jawād al-Qayyūmī, Muʾassasat Nashr al-Fiqāha, entry 6, pp. 35–36 ([p. 35](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/2684/35), [p. 36](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/2684/36)):

> `أصحابنا يقولون: إنه أول من نشر حديث الكوفيين بقم، وذكروا أنه لقي الرضا`

He names the same two books and supplies his route to them. Again there is no `ثقة`. The long argument printed in the **footnote** on p. 36—using absence of direct reports from Yūnus and al-Riḍā—is the modern editor's note, not al-Ṭūsī's text. It must have a separate author and date if ingested.

### 3.3 Al-Ṭūsī's *Rijāl*: tabaqa placement, not taʿdīl

Al-Ṭūsī, *Rijāl al-Ṭūsī*, ed. Jawād al-Qayyūmī al-Iṣfahānī, Muʾassasat al-Nashr al-Islāmī, first ed. 1415 AH, companions of al-Riḍā, entry 5224, p. 353 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/5232/353)):

> `إبراهيم بن هاشم القمي، تلميذ يونس بن عبد الرحمان`

This closely parallels the proposition that al-Najāshī attributes to al-Kashshī. Its repetition is not automatically a second independent witness. It supplies layer/association evidence and remains silent on reliability.

### 3.4 Al-Kashshī and al-Barqī controls: quoted loss, surviving selection, and silence

Al-Najāshī's `قال أبو عمرو الكشي` is a direct attribution to al-Kashshī's larger *Maʿrifat al-rijāl*, but it is not permission to manufacture a verbatim entry in the surviving *Ikhtiyār*. The searchable text and contents of al-Ṭūsī's extant selection ([complete text and contents](https://usul.ai/ar/t/ikhtiyar-macrifat-rijal)) contain Ibrāhīm b. Hāshim as a transmitter inside reports, but no located independent biographical heading or surviving wording of this proposition. The evidence record must therefore say `work = lost/full Kashshī; survival = quotation in Najāshī`, while the *Ikhtiyār* search result is only a negative control. Omission from an abridgment neither cancels al-Najāshī's attribution nor supplies jarḥ.

Al-Barqī's extant ṭabaqāt list is also a negative control. Its section `أصحاب أبي الحسن الرضا (عليه السلام) ومن نشأ في عصره` names, among others, Ibn Abī Naṣr, Ibn Bazyʿ, Ibn Faḍḍāl, Ibn Abī Najrān, Muḥammad b. Sinān, Ayyūb b. Nūḥ and al-Rayyān b. al-Ṣalt, but the normalized title `إبراهيم بن هاشم` is not located in the complete searchable transcription ([*Rijāl al-Barqī*, especially the al-Riḍā stratum](https://usul.ai/ar/t/rijal-al-barqi/7)). This is **source silence**, not a negative verdict and not proof that he did not meet al-Riḍā. The work is a transmitted ṭabaqāt list, not an exhaustive register of every transmitter and not a book in which every omission is classificatory.

These controls matter because a naïve system can make opposite errors: invent a surviving Kashshī entry from al-Najāshī's quotation, or turn al-Barqī's non-entry into `weak/not a companion`. Both exceed the sources.

### 3.5 Source-dependency consequence

The three early notices yield only two main upstream streams:

```text
Kashshī-attributed tabaqa proposition
  ├─> Najāshī: quotes it, then says "فيه نظر"
  └─> Ṭūsī, Rijāl: closely parallel title

Collective Qummī dissemination proposition ("أصحابنا يقولون")
  ├─> Najāshī, entry 18
  └─> Ṭūsī, Fihrist, entry 6
       └─> repeatedly reinterpreted by later scholars as reception/tawthīq
```

Four printed occurrences do not equal four independent early tawthīqs. None is an early explicit tawthīq at all.

## 4. The direct general certification: *Tafsīr al-Qummī*

The extant preface to *Tafsīr al-Qummī*, ed. al-Sayyid Ṭayyib al-Mūsawī al-Jazāʾirī, Dār al-Kitāb, Qum, vol. 1, printed p. 4 ([exact digital page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/5124/30); digital item 30 displays printed p. 4), says:

> `ونحن ذاكرون ومخبرون بما ينتهي إلينا ورواه مشايخنا وثقاتنا عن الذين فرض الله طاعتهم`

This is the most direct potentially classificatory evidence. Its force requires four separate findings:

1. **Attribution:** is the relevant preface attributable to ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm rather than to a later compiler of the extant composite?
2. **Grammar/scope:** does `مشايخنا وثقاتنا` mean “our trustworthy teachers,” two overlapping groups, or a looser claim about routes?
3. **Corpus membership:** is the report under review in the ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm stratum of the extant work?
4. **Person inclusion:** is Ibrāhīm, as the father's relational token, within the certified class?

For this narrator the fourth inference is unusually strong: even scholars who dispute blanket certification of every man in every chain can recognize that Ibrāhīm is a direct teacher and the dominant father-source. Al-Tustarī makes precisely this narrower point: the preface is the best item because `وأبوه أكثر من روى عنه`.

This evidence is best stored as a **general authored certification with disputed scope**, followed by a separate application edge from that class to Ibrāhīm. It must not be flattened into a fictitious individual quotation `قال علي بن إبراهيم: إبراهيم بن هاشم ثقة`.

## 5. The pivotal intermediate verdict: al-ʿAllāma al-Ḥillī

Al-Ḥillī, *Khulāṣat al-aqwāl*, ed. Jawād al-Qayyūmī, Muʾassasat Nashr al-Fiqāha, entry 9, p. 49 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/5174/49)):

> `ولا على تعديله بالتنصيص، والروايات عنه كثيرة، والأرجح قبول قوله`

This single sentence keeps three propositions distinct:

- no adverse statement located;
- no explicit textual taʿdīl located;
- abundance plus the wider file makes acceptance preferable.

`الأرجح قبول قوله` is an adjudication, not a quotation from an early source and not necessarily identical to the later technical grade `صحيح`. The modern editorial footnote on the same page gives a much later categorical argument; it cannot be attributed to al-Ḥillī.

## 6. How later major scholars reason

### 6.1 Al-Waḥīd al-Bihbahānī: an explicit cumulative-qarāʾin method

Al-Bihbahānī, *Taʿlīqa ʿalā Manhaj al-maqāl*, al-Maktaba al-Shīʿiyya text, printed pp. 57–58 ([p. 57](https://usul.ai/ar/t/tacliqa-cala-manhaj/56), [p. 58](https://usul.ai/ar/t/tacliqa-cala-manhaj/57); the URL index is one lower):

> `وفي نشره حديث الكوفيين بقم إشعار بل دلالة عليه`

He combines al-Ḥillī's grading of several Ṣadūq routes, later use of `ṣaḥīḥ`, narration by eminent figures, abundance, straightness of the reports, juristic adoption, status as a shaykh al-ijāza, and non-exclusion from the *Nawādir al-ḥikma* transmission set. His answer to the objection is historical-social: long residence in Qum, dissemination, acceptance and practice, with no Qummī criticism. On the continuation he states in substance that dissemination is not realized without reception.

Methodologically, this is not one decisive lexical token. It is **Bayesian-style accumulation before that vocabulary**: several defeasible indicators jointly cross the acceptance threshold. Each indicator must remain queryable because a user may reject one (for example, `shaykh al-ijāza`) while retaining others.

### 6.2 Al-Sayyid Mahdī Baḥr al-ʿUlūm: categorical result from five lines of inference

Baḥr al-ʿUlūm, *al-Fawāʾid al-rijāliyya*, ed. Muḥammad Ṣādiq and Ḥusayn Baḥr al-ʿUlūm, Maktabat al-Ṣādiq, vol. 1, pp. 439–465, conclusion at p. 462 ([page viewer](https://www.masaha.org/book/view/3499/page/467)):

> `والأصح عندي أنه ثقة صحيح الحديث`

His reasoning, preserved verbatim at length in al-Amīn's *Aʿyān al-Shīʿa* vol. 2 p. 236 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1371/236)), has five principal branches:

1. the son's preface plus the father's dominance in the tafsīr;
2. later explicit endorsements;
3. later scholars' grading of routes containing him as sound;
4. broad practical acceptance; and
5. Qummī reception of the disseminated Kufan material under a reportedly strict critical culture.

Branches 3–5 overlap, and later endorsements may themselves depend on the same evidence. The conclusion is clear; its independent evidence count is smaller than its argument count.

### 6.3 Al-Māmaqānī: maximal aggregation and an explicit *ḥaṣīla*

Al-Māmaqānī, *Tanqīḥ al-maqāl*, new ed., ed. Muḥyī al-Dīn al-Māmaqānī, Muʾassasat Āl al-Bayt li-Iḥyāʾ al-Turāth, Qum, vol. 5, entry 232, pp. 72–95. Key pages: [p. 83](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13795/83), [p. 84](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13795/84), [p. 92](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13795/92), [p. 94](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13795/94), [p. 95](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13795/95).

He distinguishes three received grades—ḥasan, ḥasan “like ṣaḥīḥ,” and ṣaḥīḥ—and adopts the third. His *ḥaṣīla* is unambiguous:

> `رواية إبراهيم بن هاشم من الصحيح، بالاصطلاح المتأخر أيضا بلا ريب ولا شبهة`

The dossier invokes the tafsīr preface; Ibn Ṭāwūs's evaluation of one chain; later endorsements and grading practice; status among prominent teaching/ijāza figures; abundance and sound transmission; Qummī reception; and non-exclusion from *Nawādir al-ḥikma*. On p. 94 he correctly notes the logical form: dissemination is not **lexically** an explicit tawthīq, but tawthīq may be understood without the exact word.

For dependency purposes, al-Māmaqānī is chiefly a powerful **synthesis node**, not a newly discovered early witness.

### 6.4 Al-Khūʾī: categorical tawthīq with three enumerated grounds

Al-Khūʾī, *Muʿjam rijāl al-ḥadīth*, Markaz Nashr al-Thaqāfa al-Islāmiyya edition, vol. 1, entry 332, pp. 289–322; verdict at p. 291 ([entry start](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/1/289), [verdict page](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/1/291)):

> `لا ينبغي الشك في وثاقة إبراهيم بن هاشم`

He expressly gives:

1. extensive narration by his son in a work whose preface commits to transmission through trustworthy intermediaries;
2. Ibn Ṭāwūs's `ورواة الحديث ثقات بالاتفاق` about a chain containing Ibrāhīm; and
3. being the first disseminator of Kufan ḥadīth in Qum, with Qummī reliance and no detected ghamz.

On the same page he records 6,414 isnād occurrences and says no transmitter matches him in abundance. That count is valuable corroborative corpus context, but it is not one of the three independent lexical tawthīqs.

Al-Khūʾī also performs negative corpus criticism against the Yūnus/al-Riḍā tabaqa proposition: despite roughly 160 teachers, no direct report from either is found. Meeting al-Riḍā remains possible; being his direct transmitter or Yūnus's actual student is treated as unsupported. This shows that corpus silence can rebut a claim only after a high-coverage search and an expectation model—not by a bare zero-count query.

### 6.5 Al-Tustarī: source criticism and evidence pruning

Al-Tustarī, *Qāmūs al-rijāl*, Muʾassasat al-Nashr al-Islāmī, Qum, vol. 1, entry 236, pp. 332–337 ([p. 332](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13921/332), [p. 335](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13921/335), [p. 336](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13921/336), [p. 337](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/13921/337)).

He separates chronological possibility from documented contact: Ibrāhīm reached the relevant period and layer, but meeting al-Riḍā or Yūnus is not thereby established. He also uses al-Ṣadūq's correction concerning Ḥammād b. ʿĪsā versus Ḥammād b. ʿUthmān as a concrete isnād-critical example.

His closing assessment of al-Māmaqānī's proof bundle is deliberately deflationary:

> `ولفّق المصنّف في توثيقه أمورا، أحسنها قول ابنه في أول تفسيره المعروف`

He immediately adds that the father is the person from whom the son narrates most. This is not a weakening formula about Ibrāhīm; it is an **evidence-quality ranking**. The best proof is the direct/general certification applied to the dominant teacher, while many accumulated items add less than later writers suppose. No new categorical `ثقة` formula is supplied in this entry, so the system should not manufacture one on his behalf.

### 6.6 Other consequential positions

- Mīr Dāmād treats the route through Ibrāhīm as sound and regards his stature as beyond needing another's formula; see *al-Rawāshiḥ al-samāwiyya*, p. 84 for the tabaqa discussion ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/4064/84)).
- Al-Nūrī endorses tawthīq from the Ibn Ṭāwūs sentence, the tafsīr preface, and Qummī reception; *Khātimat Mustadrak al-wasāʾil*, route 14, vol. 4, pp. 307–310, especially the reception argument ([p. 309](https://www.masaha.org/book/view/3529/page/309)).
- Muḥsin al-Amīn concludes that the man is in the highest degree of reliability and considers the long proof unnecessary because the early silence reflects obviousness, not defect; *Aʿyān al-Shīʿa*, vol. 2, p. 236 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/1371/236)). This is al-Amīn's inference after quoting Baḥr al-ʿUlūm, not another early witness.

```text
                                             └─> Khūʾī proof 2

S7 later grading ─┬─> Bihbahānī
                  ├─> Baḥr al-ʿUlūm
                  └─> Māmaqānī

S9 abundance + S10 silence ─> corroboration across later syntheses
```

### 8.3 What must not be double-counted

- Al-Najāshī and al-Ṭūsī both saying “first to disseminate” may descend from the same collective dossier.
- “Qummī acceptance,” “ability to disseminate,” “no Qummī criticism,” and “Qummī strictness” are related premises in one social-reception inference, not four independent attestations.
- Later scholars' `ṣaḥīḥ` grading may already presuppose their inferred tawthīq; using it to prove the same tawthīq can be circular.
- Al-Māmaqānī, al-Nūrī, and al-Amīn often reproduce or reorganize Baḥr al-ʿUlūm/Bihbahānī-style arguments. Repetition raises reception significance, not source independence.
- The son's preface and massive narration are distinct facts but operate together: one supplies the class predicate, the other establishes inclusion.

## 9. Adjudication

### 9.1 Strongest historical conclusion

It is historically secure that Ibrāhīm was a highly prominent conduit of Kufan material into the Qummī network and that later Imāmī transmission treated his material as central. It is also secure that the principal early biographical entries do not contain a literal individual tawthīq.

### 9.2 Strongest rijāl conclusion under cumulative inference

If the relevant *Tafsīr al-Qummī* preface is accepted for ʿAlī's stratum, applying its certification to his overwhelmingly frequent direct teacher/father is strong. Even without treating the preface as a blanket certification of every chain member, the direct-teacher application, Qummī reception, prominence, and absence of criticism form a high-confidence cumulative case. This supports the verdict **reliable by inferred tawthīq**, while retaining `explicit_tawthiq = false`.

### 9.3 Residual tawaqquf path

A strict researcher may dispute the authorship or scope of the extant preface, decline to convert dissemination into personal ʿadāla/ḍabṭ, and discount later consensus language as too late. That method should return `tawaqquf / no explicit classification`, not `ḍaʿīf`. The source record contains no early jarḥ.

### 9.4 Tabaqa question is independent

Reliability does not settle whether he directly studied with Yūnus or transmitted from al-Riḍā. Al-Najāshī's reservation, al-Khūʾī's negative corpus analysis, and al-Tustarī's chronological caution concern contact/route structure. They cannot be imported as jarḥ, and the positive tawthīq inference cannot be used to force the disputed contact.

### 10.2 Minimum evidence fields exposed by the case

Each evidentiary assertion needs at least:

- exact Arabic proposition and bounded quotation span;
- author, work, edition, volume, page, entry, and exact-page URL;
- statement speaker (`author`, `quoted_author`, `collective_report`, `modern_editor`);
- evidence kind (`explicit_tawthiq`, `general_certification`, `reception`, `frequency`, `negative_corpus`, and so on);
- target and target class;
- polarity and force;
- directness and source-dependency parent;
- application rule and scope;
- scholar accepting/rejecting the rule;
- confidence, contest status, and objection;
- corpus scope and query date for quantitative or negative claims.

### 10.3 Rules a naive database gets wrong

1. **Keyword-only classification:** no `ثقة` in an early entry does not mean weak.
2. **Quote-speaker collapse:** al-Najāshī quotes al-Kashshī and then objects; the attributed claim is not al-Najāshī's unqualified judgment.
3. **Footnote contamination:** modern editor arguments on *Fihrist* p. 36 and *Khulāṣa* p. 49 are not authorial.
4. **General-to-individual flattening:** the tafsīr preface creates a scoped class assertion, not a fabricated individual sentence.
5. **Relational-name failure:** `أبيه` requires branch-aware kinship resolution; string matching loses 3,779 candidate nodes here.
6. **Source uncertainty deletion:** `إبراهيم بن هاشم أو غيره` is explicitly disjunctive and must remain so.
7. **Repetition-as-independence:** ten later scholars repeating one inference do not create ten early attestations.
8. **Frequency-as-tawthīq:** 3,961 local nodes or al-Khūʾī's 6,414 occurrences measure prominence, not honesty by themselves.
9. **Global verdict overwrite:** al-Khūʾī's `thiqa`, al-Ḥillī's “acceptance preferable,” and a strict tawaqquf are simultaneous scholar-indexed outputs.
10. **Negative-search absolutism:** absence of direct Yūnus reports is meaningful only with declared corpus coverage and expected-frequency reasoning.
