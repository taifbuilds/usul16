# Case 1 — Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl between al-Kulaynī and al-Faḍl b. Shādhān

**Research state:** complete case-study audit; sources and local corpus checked 2026-08-24

**Occurrence under review:** al-Kulaynī → Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl → al-Faḍl b. Shādhān (الكليني → محمد بن إسماعيل → الفضل بن شاذان)

**Recommended occurrence-scoped identity:** أبو الحسن محمد بن إسماعيل البندقي/بندفر النيسابوري

**Recommended reliability state:** no early explicit tawthīq located; principled tawaqquf under an explicit-text method
**Important scope limit:** this finding applies to the recurrent al-Kulaynī → bare Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl → al-Faḍl pattern. It does not authorize a global merge of every bare محمد بن إسماعيل.

## 1. Result at a glance

Three questions must remain separate:

| Question | Result | Confidence / reason |
|---|---|---|
| Which historical person is meant in this recurrent pattern? | Most probably Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl Abū al-Ḥasan al-Naysābūrī, called al-Bunduqī/Bandafar. | Strong, not mathematically certain. Al-Kashshī directly narrates from a bare Muḥammad who narrates from al-Faḍl, elsewhere gives the Naysābūrī and fully qualified al-Bunduqī al-Naysābūrī forms, and is near al-Kulaynī in layer. Qualified control chains exclude Ibn Baziʿ and al-Barmakī. |
| Is that man explicitly declared reliable in an early biographical source? | No such declaration was located. | Al-Ṭūsī identifies him but does not say ثقة; no dedicated al-Najāshī entry was found in the searchable edition. |
| Are reports containing him therefore all unusable? | No blanket answer is justified. | Some reports have an independently acceptable parallel route; some scholars infer his reliability from cumulative indicators; other reports depend on his sole route. Each report needs its own route analysis. |

This yields a deliberately asymmetric conclusion:

- **Identity:** accept al-Naysābūrī as the best-supported occurrence-level resolution.
- **Personal reliability:** retain **tawaqquf** if the method requires an explicit or otherwise probative tawthīq.
- **Individual report validity:** decide at report level. Do not convert a parallel path in one report, or a disputed reading of al-Ṭūsī’s Mashyakha, into a blanket certification of the narrator.

The asymmetry is the point of the case. A reliable database must be able to say “identity strongly resolved, reliability unresolved.”

## 3. What the Four Books actually print

### 3.1 Literal al-Kāfī examples

The disputed name is genuinely bare at the relevant edge. It is not an editorial abbreviation for Ibn Baziʿ or al-Barmakī in these samples.

Al-Kulaynī, al-Kāfī, vol. 1, p. 31 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11005/1/31)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ عَنِ ابْنِ أَبِي عُمَيْرٍ عَنْ جَمِيلِ بْنِ دَرَّاجٍ عَنْ أَبَانِ بْنِ تَغْلِبَ

Al-Kāfī, vol. 1, p. 32 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11005/1/32)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ عَنْ حَمَّادِ بْنِ عِيسَى عَنْ رِبْعِيِّ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ

Al-Kāfī, vol. 1, p. 39 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11005/1/39)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ عَنِ ابْنِ أَبِي عُمَيْرٍ عَنْ مَنْصُورِ بْنِ حَازِمٍ

A convergent route appears in al-Kāfī, vol. 2, p. 75 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11005/2/75)):

> عَلِيُّ بْنُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ عَنْ أَبِيهِ وَ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ جَمِيعاً عَنِ ابْنِ أَبِي عُمَيْرٍ

One easily misparsed example is al-Kāfī, vol. 1, p. 36 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11005/1/36)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ يَحْيَى عَنْ أَحْمَدَ بْنِ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ عِيسَى وَ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ النَّيْسَابُورِيِّ جَمِيعاً عَنْ صَفْوَانَ

Here النيسابوري grammatically qualifies **al-Faḍl b. Shādhān**, not the preceding Muḥammad. It cannot be used as a literal qualification of the disputed name.

### 3.2 Tahdhīb and al-Istibṣār reproduce the route

Al-Ṭūsī, Tahdhīb al-aḥkām, vol. 1, p. 10 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/10083/1/10)):

> ... جَعْفَرِ بْنِ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ قُولَوَيْهِ عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ يَعْقُوبَ عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ ...

Al-Ṭūsī, al-Istibṣār, vol. 1, p. 6 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11002/1/6)):

> ... جَعْفَرِ بْنِ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ قُولَوَيْهِ عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ يَعْقُوبَ عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ ...

These are useful witnesses to textual transmission, but they are not independent evidence that identifies Muḥammad: both samples expressly pass through Muḥammad b. Yaʿqūb al-Kulaynī.

No exact محمد بن إسماعيل عن الفضل بن شاذان occurrence was found in the local Man lā yaḥḍuruhu al-faqīh corpus. Absence in that book is a bounded corpus observation, not proof of identity.

## 4. Earliest recoverable identity evidence

### 4.1 Al-Ṭūsī’s dedicated title

Al-Ṭūsī, Rijāl al-Ṭūsī, ed. Jawād al-Qayyūmī al-Iṣfahānī, Muʾassasat al-Nashr al-Islāmī, Qom, third printing 1373 Sh/1415 AH, section “those who did not narrate from the Imams,” entry 6280, p. 440 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/12146/1/440)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ، يُكْنَى أَبَا الْحَسَنِ، نَيْسَابُورِيٌّ، يُدْعَى بُنْدُقِيًّا.

The edition’s note records بندفر as a variant and points to al-Kashshī reports 1016 and 1024. This entry supplies a name, kunya, nisba, and sobriquet. It supplies **no** ثقة, مدح, or قدح.

Al-Ṭūsī’s Fihrist, ed. Muʾassasat Nashr al-Fiqāha, entry 100 under Aḥmad b. Dāwūd, p. 80 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/14010/1/80)), also says:

> وَذَكَرَ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ النِّيشَابُورِيُّ أَنَّهُ هَجَمَ عَلَيْهِ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ طَاهِرٍ ...

This confirms a Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl al-Naysābūrī associated with the same eastern scholarly material. The notice is biographical, not a tawthīq, and may depend on the same narrative stream as al-Kashshī.

### 4.2 Al-Kashshī’s direct-transmission cluster

The edition used is Ikhtiyār maʿrifat al-rijāl, the extant al-Ṭūsī recension of al-Kashshī, ed. Ḥasan al-Muṣṭafawī, Mashhad University Press, first ed. 1409 AH.

In the section on Salmān, reports 17–18, al-Kashshī transmits twice with the bare form, vol. 1, p. 8 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/10241/1/8)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ، قَالَ حَدَّثَنِي الْفَضْلُ بْنُ شَاذَانَ ...

The same direct relation occurs in the Abū Ḥamza material, report 356, p. 202 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/10241/1/202)):

> حَدَّثَنِي مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ، قَالَ حَدَّثَنَا الْفَضْلُ ...

In the Abū Yaḥyā al-Jurjānī notice, report 1016, p. 532 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/10241/1/532)), the text has:

> وَذَكَرَ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ بِنَيْسَابُورَ ...

The edition notes a variant النيسابوري. The printed بِـنَيْسَابُورَ is locative and should not be silently normalized into a nisba.

Most important is the fully qualified notice about al-Faḍl, report 1024, pp. 538–39 ([p. 538](https://lib.eshia.ir/10241/1/538), [p. 539](https://lib.eshia.ir/10241/1/539)):

> ذَكَرَ أَبُو الْحَسَنِ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ الْبُنْدُقِيُّ النَّيْسَابُورِيُّ أَنَّ الْفَضْلَ بْنَ شَاذَانَ ... نَفَاهُ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ طَاهِرٍ

No single sentence says “the bare Muḥammad in reports 17, 18, and 356 is the fully qualified man in report 1024.” The identification is an inference from a tightly connected cluster:

1. the same compiler directly receives from a bare Muḥammad;
2. that Muḥammad directly receives from al-Faḍl;
3. the compiler also names Abū al-Ḥasan Muḥammad al-Bunduqī al-Naysābūrī as the source of detailed information about al-Faḍl;
4. al-Ṭūsī independently titles the man with the same kunya, nisba, and sobriquet; and
5. al-Kashshī and al-Kulaynī occupy a near layer, making the same direct teacher structurally plausible.

That inference is strong, but its inferential character must be stored.

### 4.3 The direction of the Ibn Baziʿ relation

Al-Kashshī’s al-Faḍl notice, report 1029, p. 543 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/10241/1/543)), states:

> وَالْفَضْلُ بْنُ شَاذَانَ ... كَانَ يَرْوِي عَنْ جَمَاعَةٍ، مِنْهُمْ ... مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ بْنِ بَزِيعٍ

Thus the early documented edge is:

    al-Faḍl b. Shādhān → Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl b. Baziʿ

The disputed al-Kāfī edge is the reverse:

    bare Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl → al-Faḍl b. Shādhān

This directionality is more probative than shared name alone.

## 5. Early dossiers for the live candidates

### 5.1 Al-Barmakī, owner of the hermitage

Al-Najāshī, Rijāl al-Najāshī, ed. Mūsā al-Shubayrī al-Zanjānī, Muʾassasat al-Nashr al-Islāmī, Qom, entry 915, p. 341 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/14028/1/341)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ بْنِ أَحْمَدَ بْنِ بَشِيرٍ الْبَرْمَكِيُّ ... سَكَنَ قُمَّ ... وَكَانَ ثِقَةً مُسْتَقِيمًا

The same entry’s book route runs through Muḥammad b. Jaʿfar al-Asadī to al-Barmakī. Al-Kāfī itself makes the topology explicit:

- vol. 1, p. 78 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11005/1/78)): حَدَّثَنِي مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ جَعْفَرٍ الْأَسَدِيُّ عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ إِسْمَاعِيلَ الْبَرْمَكِيِّ الرَّازِيِّ;
- vol. 1, p. 125 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/11005/1/125)): مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ أَبِي عَبْدِ اللَّهِ عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ إِسْمَاعِيلَ الْبَرْمَكِيِّ.

Al-Barmakī is explicitly reliable. The problem is identity, not quality: al-Kulaynī’s qualified al-Barmakī occurs through an intermediary, and the qualified man is not observed narrating from al-Faḍl.

### 5.2 Ibn Baziʿ

Al-Najāshī, entry 893, pp. 330–32 ([p. 330](https://lib.eshia.ir/14028/1/330), [p. 331](https://lib.eshia.ir/14028/1/331), [p. 332](https://lib.eshia.ir/14028/1/332)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ بْنِ بَزِيعٍ أَبُو جَعْفَرٍ ... كَانَ مِنْ صَالِحِي هَذِهِ الطَّائِفَةِ وَثِقَاتِهِمْ، كَثِيرَ الْعَمَلِ

Al-Najāshī also transmits the emphatic assessment:

> ثِقَةٌ ثِقَةٌ، عَيْنٌ

and the report from al-Riḍā:

> وَدِدْتُ أَنَّ فِيكُمْ مِثْلَهُ

Al-Ṭūsī places him among al-Riḍā’s companions and says ثقة صحيح, p. 364 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/12146/1/364)). Al-Barqī lists him among those of al-Riḍā’s era, p. 54, and again in the following layer, p. 56 ([p. 54](https://lib.eshia.ir/86758/1/54), [p. 56](https://lib.eshia.ir/86758/1/56)). Al-Ṭūsī’s Fihrist gives his book of ḥajj and a route through ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm from his father, p. 215, and a second entry/book-route notice at p. 236 ([p. 215](https://lib.eshia.ir/14010/1/215), [p. 236](https://lib.eshia.ir/14010/1/236)).

Ibn Baziʿ is exceptionally well attested as reliable. He is nevertheless a poor identity match: he is an earlier-layer teacher of al-Faḍl, and qualified al-Kāfī occurrences place intermediaries between al-Kulaynī and him.

### 5.3 Al-Zaʿfarānī

Al-Najāshī, entry 933, p. 345 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/14028/1/345)):

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ بْنُ مَيْمُونٍ الزَّعْفَرَانِيُّ ... ثِقَةٌ، عَيْنٌ ... وَلَقِيَ أَصْحَابَ أَبِي عَبْدِ اللَّهِ

The explicit tawthīq made him attractive to al-Bahāʾī after Ibn Baziʿ was excluded. Meeting companions of al-Ṣādiq makes a direct al-Kulaynī relation too early, and no matching al-Faḍl edge was found in the Four Books corpus.

## 6. The candidate field and why “pick the reliable names” fails

Al-Bahāʾī’s Mishraq al-shamsayn, pp. 274–76, supplies the fullest early-modern candidate inventory ([p. 274](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/4291/7), [p. 275](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/4291/8), [p. 276](https://usul.ai/ar/t/mashriq-al-shamsayn-wa-iksir-al-saadatayn/9); the first two are exact pages, the Usul link opens the p. 276 text chunk).

Besides Ibn Baziʿ, he lists:

1. محمد بن إسماعيل بن ميمون الزعفراني
2. محمد بن إسماعيل بن أحمد البرمكي الرازي صاحب الصومعة
3. محمد بن إسماعيل بن خيثم الكناني
4. محمد بن إسماعيل الجعفري
5. محمد بن إسماعيل السلحي، وقد يقال البلخي
6. محمد بن إسماعيل الصيمري العمي
7. محمد بن إسماعيل البندقي النيسابوري
8. محمد بن إسماعيل بن رجبا الزبيدي الكوفي — thus in this digital text; other rijāl titles have ابن رجاء بن ربيعة
9. محمد بن إسماعيل بن عبد الرحمن الجعفي
10. محمد بن إسماعيل المخزومي المدني
11. محمد بن إسماعيل الهمداني
12. محمد بن إسماعيل بن سعيد البجلي

Most are only nominal candidates. Layer, geography, teacher/student direction, and qualified route forms reduce the live field:

| Candidate | Positive fit | Negative fit | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Al-Naysābūrī/Bunduqī | Direct al-Kashshī teacher; direct from al-Faḍl; full al-Faḍl-related qualification; correct layer; same regional setting | No early explicit tawthīq; bare/full identity is inferred, not stated in one sentence | Best identity match |
| Al-Barmakī | Explicitly thiqa; Rāzī like al-Kulaynī; chronological overlap is not inconceivable | Qualified al-Kāfī form is mediated; zero qualified al-Barmakī → al-Faḍl in audited corpus; al-Kashshī also reaches al-Rāzī through intermediaries | Reject as this occurrence |
| Ibn Baziʿ | Extremely strong tawthīq; famous transmitter | Too early; qualified forms are mid-chain; early edge is al-Faḍl → Ibn Baziʿ, the reverse of the disputed edge | Reject |
| Al-Zaʿfarānī | Explicit ثقة عين | Too early; no matching al-Faḍl relation | Reject |
| Other names | Shared bare name | Wrong or unknown layer, no teacher/student match, and no positive route anchor | Nominal only |

The historical temptation was to keep only explicitly trustworthy candidates, then choose between al-Barmakī and al-Zaʿfarānī. That reverses the proper order. **Identity must be resolved before reliability is inherited.**

## 7. Major later positions

### 7.1 Ṣāḥib al-Maʿālim: early source-critical narrowing

Ḥasan b. Zayn al-Dīn treats the question in the twelfth preliminary benefit of Muntaqā al-jumān, vol. 1, pp. 43–46; later quotations locate the main assessment at p. 45. He excludes Ibn Baziʿ on layer and direction, finds al-Barmakī normally mediated and al-Zaʿfarānī too early, and is left with al-Naysābūrī, whose condition remains unknown. His cautious report-level tendency is to place the affected report among the ḥasan on account of al-Kulaynī’s repeated transmission, not to fabricate an early tawthīq. A digitized full PDF is available ([non-page-specific PDF](https://hz.turathalanbiaa.com/public/430.pdf)); the exact passage is also quoted and analyzed in Jāmiʿ al-ruwāt, vol. 2, pp. 71–72 ([exact p. 71](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/3215/73), [exact p. 72](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/3215/74)).

### 7.2 Al-Bahāʾī’s al-Barmakī argument

Al-Bahāʾī first gives seven cumulative reasons why the bare man is not Ibn Baziʿ:

1. Ibn Baziʿ’s layer is too early.
2. “He reached Abū Jaʿfar II” normally marks the latest Imam reached.
3. Survival into al-Kulaynī’s teaching period would imply an extraordinary six-Imam span that biographers would likely mention.
4. Ibn Baziʿ is among al-Faḍl’s teachers, whereas the disputed man narrates from al-Faḍl.
5. A reported death during al-Jawād’s lifetime points the same way.
6. His survey finds qualified ابن بزيع only in the middle of al-Kāfī chains through intermediaries, never as the bare opening teacher.
7. Had al-Kulaynī directly met Ibn Baziʿ, one would expect high, direct reports from the Imams he met; the opening bare Muḥammad has none.

Al-Bahāʾī concedes that individual points admit objections, then gives the cumulative conclusion on p. 275:

> يَحْصُلُ مِنْ مَجْمُوعِهَا ظَنٌّ غَالِبٌ يَتَاخَمُ الْعِلْمَ ... لَيْسَ هُوَ ابْنَ بَزِيعٍ

He then filters the twelve-name list by explicit reliability. Only al-Zaʿfarānī and al-Barmakī remain; al-Zaʿfarānī is too early. He prefers al-Barmakī:

> فَيَقْوَى الظَّنُّ فِي جَانِبِ الْبَرْمَكِيِّ، فَإِنَّهُ مَعَ كَوْنِهِ رَازِيًّا كَالْكُلَيْنِيِّ فَزَمَانُهُ فِي غَايَةِ الْقُرْبِ

His answer to the mediated chains is explicit:

> الرِّوَايَةُ عَنِ الشَّيْخِ تَارَةً بِوَاسِطَةٍ وَأُخْرَى بِدُونِهَا أَمْرٌ شَائِعٌ مُتَعَارَفٌ

This is a real argument, not a careless name match. Its weakest premise is the initial reliability filter: it excludes the historically best-fitting al-Naysābūrī because no explicit tawthīq was known, then treats later ṣaḥīḥ gradings as evidence that the intended person must be one of the already-trustworthy candidates.

### 7.3 Muḥammad ʿAlī al-Ardabīlī’s defense of al-Barmakī

Jāmiʿ al-ruwāt, Muḥammad ʿAlī al-Ardabīlī, Marʿashī Library edition, vol. 2, pp. 70–72 ([exact p. 70](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/1999/72), [p. 71](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/3215/73), [p. 72](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/3215/74)):

- challenges the move from al-Naysābūrī’s “mention” of al-Faḍl’s history to direct transmission;
- argues that a narrator may be received from both directly and through an intermediary;
- uses relative proximity of al-Kulaynī, al-Asadī, and al-Barmakī; and
- concludes: ثبت أن محمد بن إسماعيل هو البرمكي.

This is the “second al-Ardabīlī” whom al-Tustarī later associates with the al-Barmakī position. It uses the same early biographies and chain forms as al-Bahāʾī; it is not an independent early witness.

### 7.4 The Ibn Baziʿ strand and its documentary limits

Later surveys attribute the Ibn Baziʿ identification to:

- al-Muḥaqqiq Aḥmad al-Ardabīlī, called “the first al-Ardabīlī” by al-Tustarī;
- apparently Ibn Dāwūd, although his cited wording raises a meeting problem rather than plainly stating a final identification;
- Shaykh ʿAbd al-Nabī al-Ḥāʾirī and others in later reports; and
- al-Sayyid Ḥasan al-Ṣadr, who devoted a manuscript to the thesis.

Al-Tustarī’s exact attribution is at Qāmūs al-rijāl, vol. 9, p. 108 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/13185/108)). I did **not** locate an exact primary passage in al-Muḥaqqiq al-Ardabīlī’s own work that states the identification; the attribution must therefore remain second-hand in this case.

Ḥasan al-Ṣadr’s title is:

> البيان البديع في أن محمد بن إسماعيل المبدوء به في أسانيد الكافي هو محمد بن إسماعيل بن بزيع

The Imam al-Ḥusayn Manuscript Heritage repository announces the manuscript ([repository notice; non-page-specific and no readable folio transcription](https://magma.imamhussain.org/arabic/331)). Since the manuscript was not directly available here, its arguments must not be quoted as if independently collated.

Al-Sayyid Muḥammad Riḍā al-Sīstānī inspected the manuscript and reconstructs its principal arguments at al-Bayān al-badīʿ manuscript pp. 7, 8–9, 10, 12, 14, and 41. His audit finds that they depend on:

- a lacuna in a copy of Kāmil al-ziyārāt that made Ibn Qulawayh appear to narrate directly from Ibn Baziʿ;
- misdivision of coordinated al-Kāfī chains as new chains beginning with Ibn Baziʿ;
- a spurious بن بزيع in a copy of the al-Faḍl route;
- an alleged Muḥammad b. Yaḥyā → Ibn Baziʿ route unsupported by the controlled copies;
- a grave-visit report with a name variant and an earlier-layer witness;
- misidentification of al-Kulaynī’s Abū Dāwūd;
- and omitted intermediaries in a citation from Taʾwīl al-āyāt.

Al-Sīstānī concludes that none establishes al-Kulaynī’s direct transmission from Ibn Baziʿ. See Qabasāt min ʿilm al-rijāl, vol. 2, pp. 472–95, especially the manuscript audit and notes 21–60 ([online transcription; non-page-specific URL](https://almerja.net/more.php?idm=205104)).

This does not erase the historical Ibn Baziʿ position. It changes its evidence status: a documented later thesis whose surviving arguments, as presently accessible, are text-critical errors rather than independent positive evidence.

### 7.5 Al-Sabzawārī’s tawaqquf

Al-Muḥaqqiq al-Sabzawārī, Dhakhīrat al-maʿād, old edition, Muʾassasat Āl al-Bayt li-Iḥyāʾ al-Turāth, vol. 1, part 1, p. 26 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/6722/26)):

> وَقَدْ يُعَدُّ إِسْنَادُهُ فِي الْكِتَابَيْنِ صَحِيحًا وَلَيْسَ بِجَيِّدٍ، إِذْ فِي الطَّرِيقِ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ الَّذِي يَرْوِي الْكُلَيْنِيُّ عَنْهُ وَهُوَ مُشْتَرَكٌ بَيْنَ الثِّقَةِ وَغَيْرِهِ ...

He concludes:

> وَبِالْجُمْلَةِ هَذَا الْإِسْنَادُ لَيْسَ عِنْدِي مِنَ الصَّحِيحِ الْمُصْطَلَحِ عَلَيْهِ، وَعَلَى هَذَا جَرَيْتُ فِي مَبَاحِثِ هَذَا الْكِتَابِ

At p. 34 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/6722/34)) he repeats:

> وَهُوَ مُشْتَرَكٌ بَيْنَ الثِّقَةِ وَغَيْرِهِ، لَكِنْ طَرِيقَةُ صَاحِبِ الْمَدَارِكِ أَنْ يَعُدَّهُ مِنَ الصِّحَاحِ

This is best classified as tawaqquf in **technical ṣaḥīḥ status and route reliance**, not a positive historical assertion that the person can never be identified. Al-Sīstānī records further applications at vol. 1 part 2 pp. 202, 213, and 270. The two exact primary pages above are already sufficient to establish al-Sabzawārī’s method.

### 7.6 Al-Māmaqānī’s eventual conclusion

Al-Māmaqānī, Tanqīḥ al-maqāl, old edition, vol. 2, section 2, entry 10387, printed pp. 80–81 ([p. 80; digital item 450](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/18291/450), [p. 81; digital item 451](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/b/18291/451)). The online host does not expose a complete original imprint on these page views.

His heading identifies:

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ أَبُو الْحَسَنِ النَّيْسَابُورِيُّ الْبَنْدُقِيُّ أَوْ بَنْدَفَر

He candidly records the adverse state of the dossier:

> صَرَّحَ جَمْعٌ بِأَنَّهُ لَمْ يَرِدْ فِيهِ تَوْثِيقٌ وَلَا مَدْحٌ ... وَقَالَ فِي الْوَجِيزَةِ إِنَّهُ مَجْهُولٌ

He then aggregates the contrary indicators: being treated as a shaykh of ijāza, the assessments of al-Fayḍ and Mīr Dāmād, al-Kulaynī’s scale of transmission, the character of the transmitted material, and later ṣaḥīḥ grading. His own bottom line is unambiguous:

> فَتَلَخَّصَ مِنْ ذَلِكَ كُلِّهِ أَنَّ الْأَقْوَى وَثَاقَةُ الرَّجُلِ وَصِحَّةُ حَدِيثِهِ

The old-edition index reduces entry 10387 to:

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ أَبُو الْحَسَنِ النَّيْسَابُورِيُّ / حَسَنٌ بَلْ ثِقَةٌ

See the index, p. 133 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/18531/133)). This is the exact classification line available in the old edition. I did not locate a separately headed new-edition حصيلة البحث for this entry, so it would be inaccurate to invent one; the quoted authorial conclusion and index grade are the recoverable eventual result.

Al-Māmaqānī also promises fuller identity discussion in an appendix benefit. The expected cross-reference could not be securely located in the digitized old-edition appendix. That is an exact source gap, not a reason to suppress his clear p. 81 conclusion.

### 7.7 Al-Tustarī’s compressed source-critical result

Al-Tustarī, Qāmūs al-rijāl, Muʾassasat al-Nashr al-Islāmī, Qom, vol. 9, p. 108 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/13185/108)):

> وَهُوَ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ الَّذِي رَوَى الْكَافِي عَنْهُ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ، فَإِنَّهُ فِي طَبَقَةِ الْكَشِّيِّ وَإِنْ أَطْلَقَهُ

He rejects the alternatives:

> وَالْقَوْلُ بِكَوْنِهِ ابْنَ بَزِيعٍ ... وَالْبَرْمَكِيَّ ... سَاقِطٌ لِإِبَاءِ الطَّبَقَةِ

His compact proof is exact:

> ابْنُ بَزِيعٍ يَرْوِي عَنْهُ الْفَضْلُ، وَهَذَا يَرْوِي عَنِ الْفَضْلِ، وَالْبَرْمَكِيُّ يَرْوِي عَنْهُ الْكُلَيْنِيُّ بِوَاسِطَةِ الْأَسَدِيِّ، وَهَذَا يَرْوِي عَنْهُ بِلَا وَاسِطَةٍ

Al-Tustarī therefore corroborates the al-Naysābūrī identity by re-running layer, direction, and intermediary tests. He does not add a new early tawthīq.

## 8. Al-Khūʾī’s full reasoning, separated by claim type

The key source is al-Khūʾī, Muʿjam rijāl al-ḥadīth, Muʾassasat al-Khūʾī al-Islāmiyya edition, vol. 16:

- entry 10261, p. 90 ([exact page](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/90));
- name-level ledger entry 10263, pp. 90–96 ([entry start](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/90));
- identity entry 10264, pp. 96–99 ([p. 96](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/96), [p. 97](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/97), [p. 98](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/98), [p. 99](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/99));
- al-Barmakī entry 10266, pp. 99–100 ([entry start](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/99));
- Ibn Baziʿ entry 10272, pp. 103–10 ([entry start](https://lib.eshia.ir/14036/16/103)).

### 8.1 Identity: why al-Naysābūrī

Al-Khūʾī’s positive chain is:

1. al-Ṭūsī supplies Abū al-Ḥasan al-Naysābūrī/Bandafar;
2. al-Kashshī directly narrates twice in the Salmān material and once in the Abū Ḥamza material from bare Muḥammad → al-Faḍl;
3. the Abū Yaḥyā material gives the Naysābūr connection;
4. the al-Faḍl biography gives the full أبو الحسن محمد بن إسماعيل البندقي النيسابوري form;
5. therefore al-Kashshī’s direct Muḥammad who transmits from al-Faḍl is that al-Naysābūrī; and
6. because al-Kashshī is close to al-Kulaynī in layer, al-Kulaynī’s prolific direct teacher of the same name and teacher relation “ينطبق على هذا.”

This is a relational identification, not an exact-name lookup.

### 8.2 Why not Ibn Baziʿ

At p. 97 al-Khūʾī calls the proposal:

> بَعِيدٌ غَايَتَهُ، بَلْ هُوَ أَمْرٌ غَيْرُ مُمْكِنٍ

His stated reason is that Ibn Baziʿ belongs to al-Riḍā’s companion layer and cannot be a direct al-Kulaynī teacher. The early direction al-Faḍl → Ibn Baziʿ makes the rejection still stronger.

### 8.3 Why not al-Barmakī

Al-Khūʾī’s negative controls are unusually concrete:

1. al-Kulaynī reaches the qualified al-Barmakī through Muḥammad b. Jaʿfar or Muḥammad b. Abī ʿAbd Allāh;
2. al-Kulaynī once spells out that Muḥammad b. Jaʿfar is al-Asadī and Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl is al-Barmakī al-Rāzī;
3. al-Ṣadūq similarly reaches al-Barmakī through al-Asadī;
4. al-Kashshī reaches the qualified al-Rāzī through Ḥamdawayh and Ibrāhīm; and
5. the qualified al-Barmakī is not found transmitting from al-Faḍl.

At p. 98:

> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ الْبَرْمَكِيُّ الْوَارِدُ فِي أَسَانِيدِ الرِّوَايَاتِ لَمْ يَرْوِ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ وَلَا فِي مَوْرِدٍ وَاحِدٍ

The conclusion “therefore definitely different” is a high-coverage corpus inference. A zero count is not metaphysical proof, but here it aligns with the positive mediated-route evidence.

### 8.4 Reliability: why identity does not become tawthīq

Al-Khūʾī identifies the man, then expressly refuses to certify him. He considers two principal arguments:

1. al-Kulaynī narrates from him hundreds of times, and al-Kāfī’s preface speaks of sound reports;
2. Ṣāḥib al-Madārik says al-Kashshī and al-Kulaynī appear to rely on him.

His answers at pp. 98–99 are methodological:

> مُجَرَّدُ إِكْثَارِ الرِّوَايَةِ عَنْ شَخْصٍ لَا يَدُلُّ عَلَى تَوْثِيقِهِ

and:

> لَا مُلَازَمَةَ بَيْنَ الْحُكْمِ بِالصِّحَّةِ وَبَيْنَ التَّوْثِيقِ

Al-Kashshī’s transmission of a historical episode is not a personal certification. Nor was it shown that al-Kulaynī issued an obligatory ruling whose only evidentiary path ran through this Muḥammad; even that would not automatically equal lexical tawthīq. His result:

> بِمِثْلِ هَذِهِ الْأُمُورِ لَا يُمْكِنُ الْحُكْمُ بِوَثَاقَةِ الشَّخْصِ

Al-Ṭūsī’s “path to him is sound” at p. 98 authenticates the route **up to** Muḥammad; it is not a statement that Muḥammad himself is reliable. Likewise, occurrence in Kāmil al-ziyārāt does not help under the later restricted view that its general certification covers Ibn Qulawayh’s direct teachers, not every man below a direct teacher.

### 8.5 Al-Khūʾī’s report-salvage argument

Al-Khūʾī then tries to reduce the practical cost of tawaqquf:

1. more than three hundred al-Kāfī cases allegedly join the Muḥammad → al-Faḍl route with ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm → his father, or with Muḥammad b. ʿAbd al-Jabbār, Muḥammad b. al-Ḥusayn, and others;
2. al-Ṭūsī’s Mashyakha presents a route to al-Faḍl through al-Kulaynī with the coordinated wording;
3. some Tahdhīb/Istibṣār reports correspond to al-Kāfī reports where only the Muḥammad route is printed; and
4. he infers that al-Kulaynī possessed multiple paths and sometimes abbreviated them.

He concludes that most, “indeed all,” of the reports can be treated as معتبرة without proving Muḥammad personally reliable.

This proposed salvage is **not** part of the identity proof and should not be stored as tawthīq.

### 8.6 Later syntactic criticism of the blanket salvage

Al-Sīstānī’s Qabasāt, vol. 2, pp. 472–95, argues that the coordinated al-Kāfī syntax was misread. In:

> عَلِيُّ بْنُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ عَنْ أَبِيهِ وَمُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِسْمَاعِيلَ عَنِ الْفَضْلِ بْنِ شَاذَانَ جَمِيعاً عَنِ ابْنِ أَبِي عُمَيْرٍ

the placement of جميعاً after al-Faḍl indicates that **Ibrāhīm b. Hāshim and al-Faḍl** both narrate from Ibn Abī ʿUmayr. If Ibrāhīm and Muḥammad both narrated from al-Faḍl, جميعاً would be expected before عن الفضل. Reversed-order examples and Ibrāhīm’s known direct relation to Ibn Abī ʿUmayr reinforce this reading.

Al-Sīstānī therefore rejects the claim that the Mashyakha supplies a second route to al-Faḍl for every lone Muḥammad report. See the argument and notes 61–71 in the [online transcription; non-page-specific URL](https://almerja.net/more.php?idm=205104).

The safe operational rule is:

- accept an actually printed parallel route for the report in which it appears;
- do not use the disputed Mashyakha syntax to manufacture an unprinted parallel route for every other report.

### 9.5 Why the historical and local counts differ

Al-Khūʾī entry 10261 says the recurrent relation reaches 761 موارد; entry 10264 speaks of around 600 reports. Elsewhere a 513 figure is reported. Al-Sīstānī says the total is under 500. The present literal al-Kāfī query finds 441 hadith records.

These figures must not be silently harmonized. Possible differences include:

- occurrence versus hadith versus expanded-chain unit;
- inclusion of derivative books or repeated paths;
- edition and segmentation differences;
- a name-level ledger being mistaken for a person-level count;
- and later editorial additions to an entry.

The current result is reproducible for this database snapshot. It does not retroactively redefine al-Khūʾī’s unit.

## 11. Adjudication

### 11.1 Identity

For the constrained occurrence pattern al-Kulaynī → bare Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl → al-Faḍl b. Shādhān, the al-Naysābūrī/Bunduqī identification is the best explanation of all positive and negative evidence.

Suggested machine state:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| identity_decision | probable_match |
| identity_target | Abū al-Ḥasan Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl al-Bunduqī/Bandafar al-Naysābūrī |
| scope | only the al-Kulaynī → Muḥammad → al-Faḍl occurrence family |
| confidence | high, not qatʿī |
| decisive features | teacher al-Faḍl; student al-Kulaynī/al-Kashshī layer; full Kashshī qualification; negative qualified controls |
| forbidden propagation | no global merge of every محمد بن إسماعيل or محمد بن إسماعيل النيسابوري |

The caution on the final line matters. Al-Sīstānī notes separate occurrences of a Muḥammad b. Ismāʿīl al-Naysābūrī in an earlier layer and answers that there can be two men with the same name and nisba. Nisba alone is therefore not a global identifier.

### 11.2 Reliability

Under an explicit-text or al-Khūʾī-style method:

| Evidence | Weight |
|---|---|
| al-Ṭūsī identity entry | identity only |
| al-Kashshī direct narration | teacher/student relation, not tawthīq |
| al-Kulaynī’s high frequency | contextual indicator; al-Khūʾī expressly rejects it as sufficient |
| al-Kāfī preface / older “ṣaḥīḥ” usage | report-selection evidence, not necessarily personal tawthīq |
| shaykh al-ijāza status | accepted by some later scholars, rejected as automatic tawthīq by stricter methods |
| later ṣaḥīḥ gradings | later adjudications, often dependent on the same identity assumptions |
| no early criticism | absence of jarḥ, not affirmative taʿdīl |

Recommended state: **reliability_unestablished / tawaqquf**, with a linked alternate scholarly verdict:

- al-Māmaqānī: ḥasan, indeed thiqa; report sound;
- al-Khūʾī: cannot establish reliability by these indicators;
- al-Sabzawārī: affected route is not technical ṣaḥīḥ;
- al-Wajīza, p. 160 ([exact page](https://ablibrary.net/book_content/9770/160)): identifies al-Naysābūrī, calls him unknown, then says his unknown status does not harm because he is among mashāyikh al-ijāza.

Do not flatten these method-indexed conclusions into one universal grade.

### 11.3 Report-level route policy

1. Parse every printed branch.
2. If a genuinely independent parallel route reaches the same downstream transmitter, evaluate that route separately.
3. Do not infer a parallel route merely because one exists in another report.
4. Do not use the disputed Mashyakha coordination to bulk-insert a second path.
5. Preserve Muḥammad’s unresolved personal status even when a particular report is validated by a different branch.

## 12. How a naïve database gets this case wrong

1. **Bare-name collapse.** It merges every محمد بن إسماعيل, including Ibn Baziʿ, al-Barmakī, al-Zaʿfarānī, and name-level Muʿjam ledgers.
2. **Reliability-first resolution.** It selects Ibn Baziʿ or al-Barmakī because they are explicitly thiqa, before testing whether either could occupy the edge.
3. **Entry-is-person assumption.** It turns Muʿjam occurrence headings 10259–63 into five duplicate people, exactly as the present candidate resolver demonstrates.
4. **Frequency-as-tawthīq.** It converts hundreds of al-Kulaynī reports into an invented personal declaration.
5. **Unit confusion.** It describes 893 broad nodes, 779 hadiths, 441 literal-pair hadiths, 232 parsed-pair hadiths, and 294 expanded chains as if they were the same count.
6. **Derivative inflation.** It counts Tahdhīb and al-Istibṣār reproductions through al-Kulaynī as independent biographical witnesses.
7. **Direction blindness.** It treats al-Faḍl → Ibn Baziʿ as support for Ibn Baziʿ → al-Faḍl.
8. **Qualifier attachment error.** It assigns النيسابوري in al-Kāfī 1:36 to Muḥammad rather than to al-Faḍl.
9. **Coordination error.** It attaches جميعاً to the wrong junction and fabricates a second route to al-Faḍl.
10. **Blanket Mashyakha expansion.** It inserts an unprinted alternate path into every lone occurrence.
11. **Tawaqquf-as-null.** It treats “identity strong, reliability not proven” as missing data instead of a substantive scholarly result.
12. **Global propagation.** It exports the occurrence-level al-Naysābūrī resolution to unrelated bare-name chains.

## 15. Exact remaining gaps

1. The original passage in al-Muḥaqqiq Aḥmad al-Ardabīlī that allegedly selects Ibn Baziʿ was not located. The position is retained only as an attribution by al-Tustarī and later surveys.
2. Ḥasan al-Ṣadr’s manuscript was not available as a readable folio set. Its arguments are reported through al-Sīstānī’s direct manuscript audit; the repository page is only a notice.
3. A separately headed new-edition حصيلة البحث for al-Māmaqānī entry 10387 was not found. The old-edition authorial conclusion and index grade are exact and have been quoted; no modern editorial verdict is manufactured.
4. Al-Māmaqānī’s promised appendix discussion could not be securely identified in the searchable old-edition appendix.
5. Complete imprint data were not exposed for every hosted classical edition, especially al-Barqī and the old Tanqīḥ. Missing metadata are left missing.
6. “Not found” source absences are bounded to the named searchable editions and the 2026-08-24 audit. They are not claims about unexamined manuscripts.
7. The local corpus is a project snapshot. Its 441 exact al-Kāfī records should be regenerated after any extraction, segmentation, or source-edition change.

The resulting uncertainty is not a defect to be patched away. It is the correct scholarly output: **a strongly resolved occurrence identity, no recovered early explicit tawthīq, and report-level rather than blanket authentication.**
